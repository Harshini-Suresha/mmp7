Background and context

Why MMP-7 is a target, and why the zinc-binding group matters.

A validated but hard target

The MMP family has at least 28 members, and MMP-1, -2 and -7 are among those experimentally validated as cancer targets. MMP-7 (matrilysin) degrades extracellular-matrix components, and its dysregulated activity is linked to cancer, inflammatory disease and fibrosis.

The hydroxamate legacy

Early broad-spectrum inhibitors used a hydroxamate zinc-binding group to bind the catalytic zinc strongly. They failed in clinical trials; musculoskeletal pain was a common side effect, and lack of selectivity is the usual explanation. Prinomastat, for example, failed a Phase III lung-cancer trial.

The selectivity problem

MMP-1 and MMP-7 have similar S1′ hydrophobic pockets, which makes selective MMP-7 inhibition difficult. Researchers have explored other zinc-binding groups, including a nitro group, and side-chain changes to favour MMP-7 over MMP-1.

Where this project fits

This screen asks how well docked poses reproduce the hydroxamate chelation geometry seen in the crystal structure, and how that compares with other chemotypes. Only MMP-7 was docked, so selectivity across the family is outside what these results can show.

The catalytic zinc machinery

In PDB 1MMQ the catalytic Zn²⁺ is held by three histidines (His218, His222, His228 of the HEXXHXXGXXH motif) plus both hydroxamate oxygens at 2.16 and 2.20 Å, with Glu219 hydrogen-bonding the N–OH. Any credible pose must satisfy this geometry, not just score well.

Why it still matters

MMP-7 remains a biomarker and therapeutic candidate in cancer and kidney fibrosis, with recent high-resolution structures (e.g. 8JUD/8JUF/8JUG, 1.3–1.5 Å) renewing interest in selective, non-hydroxamate chemotypes. That is why this screen judges affinity and zinc-chelation fidelity as separate evidence.

Sources: RSC Advances 2015, nitro-based MMP-7 inhibitors · Dove Press review of MMP inhibitors · Patsnap Synapse overview of MMP-7 inhibitors (a general overview, less rigorous than the other two).

Five donors (three histidine NE2 atoms and both hydroxamate oxygens). The geometry index τ5 is 0.16, so the site is a distorted square pyramid (0 is an ideal square pyramid, 1 an ideal trigonal bipyramid). Docked poses can only be compared with this geometry once their coordinates are available; the screen here records O–Zn distances.

How to run your own screen

The same pipeline used for the results above, step by step.

1 Get the structure

PDB 1MMQ (matrilysin with a hydroxamate inhibitor). The script downloads it, keeps chain A with Zn²⁺ and Ca²⁺, and centres the grid box on the co-crystallised inhibitor.

2 Collect ligands

Make a CSV with name,smiles. This screen used the 50 most potent known MMP-7 actives from ChEMBL target CHEMBL4073 (best pChEMBL per molecule).

3 Dock

python dock_mmp7.py --pdb 1MMQ --ligands ligands_mmp7.csv --exhaustiveness 8 --confs 3

Needs Open Babel, RDKit, Meeko and Vina. Each ligand is minimized, docked, and its best pose is measured against the catalytic zinc.

4 Analyze

Paste lab_paste.txt into the Docking Lab above for a ranked chart. Read results.csv for O–Zn distances and contact flags.

5 Inspect interactions

Open poses/<ligand>_complex.pdb in PyMOL. For residue-level contacts run plip -f poses/<ligand>_complex.pdb -t or use LigPlot.

6 Interpret

Judge affinity and Zn²⁺ geometry as separate evidence, using the guide below.

How to read the outputs

ColumnMeaningHow to read it
affinity_kcal_molBest Vina pose scoreMore negative means stronger predicted binding. Use it to rank, not as an absolute energy.
min_O_Zn_AClosest ligand oxygen to the catalytic Zn²⁺In the 1MMQ crystal structure the hydroxamate oxygens sit at 2.16 and 2.20 Å. Values near 2.2–2.4 Å resemble real chelation.
n_O_within_cutoffOxygens within 2.6 Å of Zn²⁺Counts how many oxygens reach the metal.
zn_contact1 if at least one oxygen is within 2.6 ÅA pose that touches the zinc at all.
bidentate_like1 if two or more oxygens are within 2.6 ÅThe two-point chelation seen for hydroxamates.

Worked example: CHEMBL41497

Best score of the 50 (−9.25 kcal/mol), closest oxygen 2.3 Å from Zn²⁺, and two oxygens within the cutoff, so it is bidentate-like. That matches the crystal chelation distances of 2.16 and 2.20 Å, so the pose reproduces the expected binding mode.

Counter-example: CHEMBL5572317

Second-best score (−9.05) but its closest oxygen is 2.65 Å from the zinc, just outside the cutoff, so it is not counted as a contact. A strong score does not guarantee chelation, which is why both are reported.

Glossary

Terms used on this page.

Affinity (Vina score)
The predicted binding free energy from AutoDock Vina in kcal/mol. More negative means stronger predicted binding. It is best used for ranking, not as an exact energy.
Zinc-binding group (ZBG)
The part of an inhibitor that coordinates the catalytic zinc, such as a hydroxamate or carboxylic acid.
Hydroxamate
A C(=O)N–OH group. It chelates zinc with both oxygens, which is why hydroxamates are strong MMP inhibitors.
Bidentate chelation
Binding a metal through two donor atoms at once. Here, a pose is called bidentate-like when two ligand oxygens lie within 2.6 Å of the zinc.
O–Zn distance
The distance from a ligand oxygen to the catalytic zinc. In the 1MMQ crystal structure the hydroxamate oxygens sit at 2.16 and 2.20 Å.
Heavy atom
Any non-hydrogen atom. Heavy-atom counts here come from the SMILES strings.
Ligand efficiency
|Affinity| divided by the number of heavy atoms: binding strength per atom, which corrects for ligand size.
Pareto front
The set of ligands that no other ligand beats on both of two chosen metrics at once.
τ5 index
A measure of five-coordinate geometry: 0 is an ideal square pyramid, 1 an ideal trigonal bipyramid.
S1′ pocket
A hydrophobic pocket next to the catalytic zinc that accommodates the inhibitor side chain and helps determine selectivity between MMPs.
pChEMBL
A standardised potency value from ChEMBL: −log10 of the molar IC50, Ki or Kd. Higher means more potent.
PLIP / LigPlot
Tools that list or draw protein–ligand interactions (hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, salt bridges) from a complex structure.
B-factor
A crystallographic measure of atomic displacement or disorder. Higher values mean a more mobile or less certain position.
Ramachandran plot
A plot of backbone φ and ψ angles for each residue, used to check backbone geometry.
